Privacy

Last updated 19 August 2026

Oracle Party is a small trivia game. This page says exactly what it stores, who else can see it, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read, not to be skipped.

The short version

  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party trackers of any kind.
  • Nothing is ever sold or shared for marketing.
  • You can play without an account at all.
  • If you make an account, you can delete it and everything attached to it, yourself, at any time.

If you play as a guest

Without an account, the game stores:

  • The display name you type. It is kept in your browser and shown to the other people in your room.
  • What happens in the game — the answers you type, your wagers, your score, and any chat messages or honks you send. Everyone in your room sees these, because that is the game.
  • A random ID stored in your browser. It is not linked to you, your name or your device. It exists so that rating a question counts once per person rather than once per tap.

When a game finishes and everyone leaves, the room and its answers are deleted. Chat is archived with the room's code and the display names used in it.

If you make an account

In addition to the above:

  • Your email address and password, or, if you choose "Continue with Google", the name, email address and profile picture Google gives us. We never see your Google password.
  • Your profile — display name, avatar colour and emoji, an optional short bio, a favourite category, your chosen title, and whether you want your online status shown.
  • Your playing record — which questions you have seen and whether you got them right, your finished games and where you placed, and the titles you have unlocked. This is what the leaderboards, stats and mastery tree are built from.
  • Your friends — friend requests you send or receive, and who you are friends with.

What other players can see

Your display name, avatar, title and tier, and anything you type in a game or in chat. If you appear on a leaderboard, your name and your totals are public to anyone who opens it. You can turn off your online status in your profile.

Who else is involved

Four services, and none of them are advertisers:

  • Supabase stores the database and handles sign-in. Your account and game data live there.
  • GitHub Pages serves the website itself.
  • Google Fonts supplies the typefaces.
  • esm.sh supplies one piece of the code the game runs on.

The last three see your IP address simply because your browser has to ask them for a file — that is true of every website. Google additionally sees your sign-in if, and only if, you choose to sign in with Google.

How long it is kept

Your account and playing record are kept until you delete them, because that is what makes stats, leaderboards and titles work at all. Rooms and the answers inside them are deleted when the game ends and everyone leaves.

Deleting everything

Open your Profile, scroll to the bottom, and use Delete Account. It removes your account, profile, stats, game history, question history, titles, friends and friend requests, and any question ratings you gave. It cannot be undone, and you will not be able to sign in again afterwards.

Two things deliberately survive, and neither identifies you once your account is gone:

  • A count of games played in each category, which records the display name used at the time but is no longer connected to any account.
  • Archived chat from rooms you played in, stored under the display name used at the time.

Totals about the questions themselves — how often a question is answered correctly, and which answers people commonly type — are kept, and contain no record of who gave them.

Age

You must be at least 13 to use Oracle Party, or older if the law where you live sets a higher age. The game is not intended for younger children and does not knowingly keep their information.

Security, honestly stated

Treat chat and anything you type in a game as public. The game runs entirely in players' browsers with no server of its own, and guests do not sign in — so there is no way for the database to tell who is asking for what. A person who went looking could read messages from a room they were never in. This is a known limitation, not a secret, and fixing it properly means moving the game's logic onto a server, which is planned.

One thing we have changed in the meantime: joining a room now shows you the conversation from the moment you arrived, not everything said before it. That means somebody who walks in off the public games list, or with a room code, does not get handed the whole history. It is a courtesy rather than a lock — it does not change the paragraph above.

Your password is a different matter: sign-in is handled by Supabase and the game itself never sees or stores it. Signing in with Google means we never see that password either.

Oracle Party is a small hobby project, not a bank. Please do not put anything sensitive in a display name, a bio or a chat message.
